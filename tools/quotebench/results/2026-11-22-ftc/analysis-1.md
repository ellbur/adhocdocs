# How the agreement not to deal with buying groups was proved, or not, against each distributor

*In re Benco Dental Supply Co., Henry Schein, Inc. & Patterson Cos.*, FTC Dkt. No. 9379
Prepared for the partner meeting. Working draft; not reviewed by a lawyer.

---

## Bottom line

- **Benco and Patterson: liable.** The ALJ (Chief ALJ D. Michael Chappell) held that "Complaint Counsel has met its burden of proving that Benco and Patterson conspired to refuse to discount to or otherwise compete for the business of buying groups" and that the agreement is per se unlawful (Initial Decision, Conclusions of Law ¶¶ 31, 36, 38; `d09379bencoinitialdecisionpublic.pdf`).
- **Schein: dismissed.** "Complaint Counsel has failed to meet its burden of proving a conspiracy involving Schein" (*id.*, CoL ¶ 32; § II.E.6).
- **The difference came down to the documents.** Against Patterson there was a written exchange between the two top executives, Cohen ("Our policy at Benco is that we do not recognize, work with, or offer discounts to buying groups") and Guggenheim ("We feel the same way about these"). That exchange was followed by a written confrontation and a written reassurance (ID § II.D.1). Against Schein the ALJ found no proof of an exchange of assurances at all. He found Benco's later contacts "unsolicited" and "rebuffed," and he found that Schein kept doing business with buying groups throughout the period (ID §§ II.E.1–4).

## The legal frame the ALJ applied (briefly)

- **Standard.** Preponderance of the evidence. "An inference of conspiracy must be more likely than an inference of independent action" (ID § II.E.6, p. ~96–97, citing *Kreuzer*).
- **Complaint counsel's theory.** Benco, which had a no-buying-group policy since the mid-1990s, used executive-to-executive "exchanges of assurances" to obtain separate agreements from Patterson and from Schein. The parties then complied, monitored each other, and "confronted one another over perceived deviations" (ID § II.B.4, p. 15–16, summarizing CCB at 2). Complaint counsel also alleged an "overarching" three-way agreement (ID § II.E.5).
- **The ALJ's frame.** Evidence of an exchange of assurances, then confrontation about cheating, then reassurance, can prove agreement. So can conduct conforming to a course of action a competitor proposed. Witness denials get "little weight" where documents show agreement (ID § II.D.3). Opportunity to conspire, motive, and an oligopoly's awareness of its rivals do not prove agreement by themselves (ID § II.E.5.c; CoL ¶¶ 27–29).

## 1. Benco: the policy, and why its explanation didn't hold

Benco's liability depends entirely on the Patterson findings. The decision analyzes Benco's own conduct mainly as the hub.

| Evidence | Complaint counsel | Benco | ALJ |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Benco's no-buying-group policy** (since 1996) | Benco feared its policy "would be futile" unless Patterson and Schein adopted it too, so it communicated with them to "ensure that Patterson and Schein agreed to the same" (ID p. 15–16, quoting CCB at 2). | The policy is unilateral and longstanding. Benco "refused to do business with buying groups as a single customer before, during and after the alleged conspiracy" (ID § II.C, p. 17, quoting BB at 1). | Undisputed. A "blanket rule," enforced by Cohen and Ryan (Ryan: "We all know the answer here, right? Not no, hell no.") (ID § II.C, p. 16–17). The policy alone is not the violation. The question is whether Benco "obtained the agreement of Patterson and Schein" (p. 17). |
| **Why Cohen told Guggenheim the policy** (Feb. 8, 2013) | An assurance, given to obtain one in return. | Cohen "did not recall why he included an FYI about Benco's policy… 'It seemed to be germane to the topic, but no special reason.'" The policy was "not confidential and it was not an industry secret" (BB p. 30). | "Benco did not persuasively explain why Cohen shared Benco's policy." If the policy were public, telling Guggenheim would have been "unnecessary," which "undercut[s] Benco's stated reason" (ID p. 23). Cohen's own words, "I like to give information to get information," were read as "seeking to establish a quid pro quo" (ID p. 23). |
| **Benco's communications overall** | Benco reached out to both rivals repeatedly. | "The only communications that Chuck Cohen has ever even had with anyone at Patterson about buying groups is limited to two brief e-mail exchanges… The only communications… with anyone at Schein… is limited to one exchange with Tim Sullivan regarding Atlantic Dental Care" (BB p. 35–36). | The ALJ accepted that the Patterson exchanges were assurances. On Schein, the ALJ found Benco's outreach "raises concerns" but proved nothing against Schein (ID § II.E.6). |

**Small dating errors in Benco's briefs.** BRB p. 19 dates the Guggenheim email "February 2012"; the decision and every other brief say February 8, 2013. BB dates Cohen's ADC email from Ryan "March 25, 2015" and his follow-up text "March 27, 2017"; elsewhere both are 2013. These look like typos, but they are worth knowing if any of those passages gets quoted.

## 2. Patterson: how the agreement was proved

### 2.1 The February 8, 2013 exchange (NMDC): the central document

**The words** (ID p. 20–21, 26, quoting the exhibit; CCB p. 30 cites it as CX0056):
- Cohen → Guggenheim: *"Just wanted to let you know about some noise I've picked up from New Mexico. FYI: Our policy at Benco is that we do not recognize, work with, or offer discounts to buying groups (though we do work with corporate accounts) and our team understands that policy."*
- Guggenheim → Cohen: *"Thanks for the heads up. I'll investigate the situation. We feel the same way about these."*

**Complaint counsel.** "Patterson's Guggenheim agreed: 'we feel the same way about these.' Within three days, Patterson abandoned the buying group arrangement that Cohen had brought to Guggenheim's attention" (CCB p. 13). In reply: "Cohen and Guggenheim both admitted that, following this communication, they each understood that the other would not discount to buying groups as a matter of 'policy'" (CCRB p. 22). Patterson's brief "dismisses this critical communication as 'a 10-second email,' but the length of time it took to type out the words is irrelevant" (CCRB p. 104).

**Patterson.**
- It was "an out-of-the-blue email" that "did not ask Guggenheim to do anything, let alone commit to anything." Guggenheim "simply and quickly responded in a '10-second email'… his throwaway comment… was not a commitment of any kind" (PB p. 14).
- "'I'll investigate' is not an explicit, unambiguous statement of agreement"; the reply "shared Patterson's existing feeling or policy," which "is not direct evidence of an illegal agreement" (PB p. 46, citing *Blomkest*).
- Complaint counsel's own expert, Dr. Marshall, "conceded that a 'feeling' was not a 'commitment' or an 'agreement'" (PB p. 14–15; PRB p. 14).
- Guggenheim at trial: "Did you commit in any way to Mr. Cohen in this e-mail that Patterson was not going to discount to buying groups? A. Absolutely not." (PRB p. 22, quoting Guggenheim, Tr. 1707.)

**Benco.** Cohen "did not write the February 8, 2013 e-mail for the purpose of forming any agreement… The email did not ask Guggenheim or Patterson to do anything… Mr. Cohen never followed up" (BB p. 30). Exchanging information is lawful "where the parties then make independent business decision[s]" (BB p. 31, citing *Michelman*).

**ALJ.**
- The exchange "is persuasive proof of an exchange of assurances of a common course of action" (ID p. 26).
- Timing undercut Patterson's "10-second" account. Guggenheim forwarded Cohen's email to his sales and pricing heads, Misiak and Rogan, about 20 minutes after receiving it, and replied about two hours later, which "tends to undermine the argument that Guggenheim's response was just a quick response" (ID p. 25).
- Guggenheim "confirmed at trial that his response meant that Patterson felt the same way as Benco," and Cohen read it that way: "That's the way I interpreted that sentence. Yes." (ID p. 25–26).
- "Guggenheim could not identify any business rationale" for the reply (ID p. 26).
- The ALJ rejected the "public knowledge" defense, relying on *United States v. FMC Corp.*: an exchange of publicly known information can still evidence a meeting of the minds (ID p. 23–24).

> **Characterization difference.** Patterson calls the reply a statement of an *existing* "feeling," not a commitment about future conduct (PB p. 46). Complaint counsel and the ALJ read it as an assurance about future conduct, since sharing a position on buying groups "conveys its future bidding strategy" (ID p. 26).

### 2.2 Patterson's withdrawal from NMDC three days later

**What the decision found** (ID p. 21–22, 27):
- On February 7, 2013, Patterson's Albuquerque branch manager wrote that NMDC "has the opportunity to be huge… I definitely want to keep this moving forward."
- On February 8, Cohen and Guggenheim exchanged their emails.
- On February 11, the regional manager told NMDC that Patterson would not work with it.
- "There is no testimony in the record from any of the Patterson representatives who attended the February 11 meeting" (ID p. 22).

**Complaint counsel.** "Within three days, Patterson abandoned the buying group arrangement" (CCB p. 13).

**Patterson.**
- "NMDC's Brenton Mason testified that Patterson started pulling back from the deal after he sent an industry-wide email blast on February 4, and that the pullback was apparent by February 7." There is "no evidence that anyone at Patterson's corporate office (in Minnesota) instructed Patterson's local sales team (in Albuquerque) over the weekend" (PB p. 58).
- Dr. Marshall admitted, "I have nothing to point to that would say Mr. Guggenheim made a transmission directly to the managers in New Mexico" (PB p. 15).
- Guggenheim never investigated and never got back to Cohen (PB p. 14).
- Separately, "NMDC did not consider itself a buying group" (PB, ¶ 282 as summarized).

**Benco.** "There is no evidence that Mr. Cohen's email had any impact on Patterson." NMDC's dentists went on to buy from Schein through the Utah co-op (BB p. 30–31).

**ALJ.** Patterson's "sudden termination of discussions with NMDC was attributable to a direction from Guggenheim, Misiak, and/or Rogan." That is an inference, drawn from the sequence: "keep this moving forward" on February 7, the exchange and the forwarding to Misiak and Rogan on February 8, termination on February 11 (ID p. 27). It "evidences action taken in conformance with the course of action suggested by Benco," which "implies an agreement" (*id.*, citing *Paramount*, *Interstate Circuit*).

> **Characterization difference, and the point most open to attack.** The ALJ acknowledged there is no direct evidence of an instruction from headquarters and inferred one. Patterson reads the February 7 emails as already "pulling back," because the branch manager cancelled the March 13 vendor meeting that day. The ALJ read the same emails as still "moving forward." Both readings rest on the same February 7 emails (ID p. 21–22).

### 2.3 Patterson declines ADC (February 27, 2013)

**The words** (ID p. 28; the decision's quotation reproduces the original's typo, "we do [not]"):
- Fruehauf (regional manager) → Misiak: "what it could mean if we set a precedent of offering lower prices to groups such as this."
- Misiak → Fruehauf: "When I get these calls directly I politely say that I appreciate the opportunity, but currently we do [not] participate with group purchasing organizations. Continue to help Devon stay out of this with grace."
- Misiak, the same day (ID p. 97): "**Confidential and not for discussion** . . . our 2 largest competitors stay out of [buying groups] as well. If you hear differently and have specific proof please send that to me."
- Misiak → Guggenheim: he was "concerned that Schein and Benco sneak into these [buying group] bids and deny it."

**Complaint counsel.** A Patterson executive "confidently instructed his team to reject a buying group knowing that: '[O]ur 2 largest competitors stay out of these as well'" (CCB p. 14). Talk of rivals "sneaking into bids" is talk of cheating, which "establishes a conscious commitment to a common scheme" (CCRB, around p. 115).

**Patterson.** Patterson decided not to bid on ADC "on its own," months before the June emails (PB p. 15, 46). The "2 largest competitors" email "does not discuss any agreement with Benco or Schein to do anything" (PRB p. 55). Misiak "was simply fishing for market intelligence" and "strongly denied that he was referring to an agreement" (PRB p. 52).

**ALJ.**
- As Patterson evidence, Misiak's email is "clearly suggestive of an instruction to avoid buying groups" (ID p. 42).
- As evidence against Schein, the "confidential and not for discussion" language and the words "sneak" and "deny" are "suggestive of the type of secretiveness and monitoring that can be consistent with conspiracy." But Misiak "acknowledged that his statement that Schein 'staying out of' buying groups was speculation." Covert competition for customers "is consistent with normal competition" (ID p. 97).

### 2.4 The June 2013 ADC exchange: confrontation and reassurance

**The words** (ID p. 35–36; BB p. 35 quotes it identically; CCB cites CX0062):
- Guggenheim → Cohen, June 6, 2013. He replied on top of Cohen's February email and blind-copied Misiak, Rogan and Nease: *"Reflecting back on our conversation earlier this year, could you shed some light on your business agreement with Atlantic Dental Care?… I'm wondering if your position on buying groups is still as you articulated back in February? Let me know your thoughts….Sometimes these things grow legs without our awareness."*
- Cohen → Guggenheim, June 8: *"As we've discussed, we don't recognize buying groups."* He then gave the reasons Benco judged ADC not to be a buying group (merged ownership, rebranding, a board) and added: "[W]e're going to continue monitoring the process to ensure that ADC delivers on their commitment to us."
- Guggenheim, June 10: "Sounds good Chuck. Just wanted to clarify where you guys stand." He forwarded the exchange to Patterson's CEO and told the branch manager, "It's a little grey but I guess he has a point" (ID p. 37).

**Complaint counsel.** Guggenheim "confronted Cohen about Benco's doing business with ADC… and Cohen reassured Guggenheim that Benco was complying with their agreement" (ID p. 19, summarizing CCB at 51–52). Guggenheim's admission at trial: "It's fair to say that you viewed Benco's doing business with Atlantic Dental Care as a deviation from what Chuck Cohen had told you before about Benco's policy? A. Yes." (CCRB n.1305, Guggenheim, Tr. 1628).

**Patterson.** The email "contains no reference to any past agreement to take any concerted action, nor does it contain any commitment to take any future concerted action." It came "months after Patterson had decided on its own not to bid," and after ADC had already chosen Benco. "After-the-fact discussions of decisions already made do not violate antitrust laws" (PB p. 46, citing *Blomkest*). It also shows the two companies "viewed ADC in very different ways (and, thus, had no agreement)" (PB p. 15).

**Benco.** Guggenheim was trying "to gain information that would better allow Nease to compete" ("I wanted to see what intelligence I could find out to help Devon get back in there and compete," Guggenheim IHT). "Because Benco had already won the ADC contract, Mr. Cohen saw no harm in sharing Benco's thinking" (BB p. 36).

**ALJ.** This was a "confrontation about perceived cheating followed by reassurance of conduct, which removes the ambiguity as to whether the February 2013 exchange of assurances formed an agreement" (ID p. 38).
- It is "not credible that Guggenheim would contact his competitor, finding and responding to a four month-old email describing Benco's no buying group policy… if Guggenheim was simply seeking information about ADC's structure" (ID p. 37).
- *Blomkest* was distinguished because this exchange concerned Benco's policy, "which impacts Benco's future bidding strategy" (ID p. 36–37).
- Cohen's detailed explanation "supports the inference that Cohen felt obligated to explain a perceived shift" (ID p. 37, citing *Twombly*'s "sense of obligation").

> **Characterization difference.** The ALJ treats the fact that Patterson stayed out of ADC while Benco bid (because they classified ADC differently) as conduct *consistent with* a shared rule applied to differing classifications (ID p. 39–40). Patterson treats the same divergence as proof that there was *no* agreement (PB p. 15).

### 2.5 Patterson's internal statements after February 2013

| Statement (as quoted in ID) | Complaint counsel | Patterson | ALJ |
|---|---|---|---|

(ID p. 42) | The companies' "contemporaneous records attest to their 'agreement' and a 'duty to uphold'" it (CCB p. 12, 14). | Rogan "was simply writing based on his observations in the market" (PRB p. 52). | As Patterson evidence: "clearly suggestive of an instruction to avoid buying groups" (ID p. 42). As Schein evidence: it was Rogan's "opinion ('[m]y two cents')," "our duty" meant Patterson's senior leadership, and the same email chain showed Schein working with a buying group (ID p. 95–96). |

(ID p. 43) | "A prior commitment against such conduct" (CCB p. 14). | Complaint counsel concedes no signed agreement exists. McFadden "made this statement, untruthfully, to avoid a former employee whom he had fired." My guidance has been to politely say no [to buying groups]" (ID p. 42) | Instructions carrying out the agreement. | Patterson was "always skeptical" of buying groups and had turned them down before 2013 (PB, as summarized ID p. 40–41). | Directions from executives "clearly suggest that, following the February 2013 exchange… Patterson instructed its employees not to do business with buying groups" (ID p. 42). |

### 2.6 Patterson's conduct: "summarily rejected… without evaluation"

- **Patterson's position.** Patterson evaluated buying groups and rejected them for legitimate reasons, and its branches remained free to deal (PB p. 15–19, as summarized ID p. 40). It cited deals it says were with buying groups (Jackson Health, Orthosynetics, a 2017 Smile Source bid) and reasons for its rejections: a "vig," a veterinarian, a representative it found to be lying.
- **The ALJ's response.** He worked through each example against the contemporaneous documents:
  - Jackson Health was "not a GPO" according to Patterson's own branch manager.
  - McFadden said Orthosynetics was "not a buying club per se."
  - On Dental Purchasing Group, Guggenheim wrote: "Typical approach of an upstart buying group. We pass on these as a matter of protecting our business model."
  - The 2017 bid fell outside the period (ID p. 44–46).
  - Special Markets' founding definition stated: "This definition will not include group purchasing organizations ('GPOs')" (ID p. 43).
- **The finding.** Patterson "summarily rejected them without evaluation, resulting in a near blanket refusal" (ID p. 49).
- **Before and after.** Before February 2013, Patterson had "no company policy" on buying groups, and decisions were made at the branches (ID p. 40). After April 2015, Patterson began studying buying groups as a market, and its own examples "actually show that Patterson's approach to buying groups did change" (ID p. 47–48).
- **Legitimate business reasons don't change the result.** Even if Patterson had them, they do "not outweigh the evidence of the exchange of assurances" (ID p. 46–47, citing *General Motors* and *Apple*). An agreement to keep a pre-existing course of action is still an agreement (ID p. 41, citing *North Dakota Hosp. Ass'n*).

### 2.7 Denials

- **Benco and Patterson.** Every fact witness denied an agreement, and sworn denials are direct evidence under *McWane* (ID p. 50, summarizing BB at 7 and PB at 3–4, 23–26, 38).
- **ALJ.** *McWane* is distinguishable. There, "there was no evidence about the content of the communications." Here the content is in writing. The denials are "given little weight" (ID p. 50–51, citing *U.S. Gypsum*).

## 3. Schein: why the same theory failed

Complaint counsel's theory against Schein had the same four parts: (1) an exchange of assurances in 2011, (2) compliance (a change to "categorically" rejecting buying groups), (3) Benco confronting Schein over deviations, and (4) Benco reassuring Schein. The ALJ found each part unproved.

### 3.1 The alleged 2011 exchange of assurances

**Complaint counsel.**
- Cohen and Sullivan "called each other at least 23 times, texted each other at least 89 times" in 2011 (CCB p. 38).
- Cohen testified: "Q. You did communicate Benco's no-buying group policy to Mr. Sullivan; correct? A. I believe I did. Yes." (CCRB n.1305; ID p. 47).
- Cohen's investigational-hearing account of Sullivan's position (CCB p. 37–38): "if you go back to the wrath [sic] of text messages… I think that the policy that Henry Schein had was that they do not recognize GPOs."

**Schein.** No communication about buying groups in 2011 exists. The calls concerned the companies' hiring agreement, and the texts are innocuous (ID p. 46–47, summarizing SRB at 22–23). "The only direct evidence of whether there was a conspiracy is the sworn denials of each and every fact witness" (SB p. 12).

**Benco.** No proof of any 2011 communication about buying groups (ID p. 46, summarizing BB at 14–16).

**ALJ.** Not proved.
- "Complaint Counsel does not cite to any communications between Schein and Benco relating to buying groups during or before 2011." Many of the calls "lasted mere seconds," and "their content will not be presumed" (ID p. 46–47).
- The texts cover sports, charity, and the hiring agreement ("I'm going to Yankee Stadium for game 5 tomorrow nite. Go Yanks!") (ID p. 47).
- Cohen's "I believe I did" was contradicted by Sullivan's consistent denials at trial, in his IHT and in his deposition. Cohen himself said the only buying-group communication with Sullivan was in 2013. Complaint counsel "provides no persuasive reason to credit Cohen's testimony over Sullivan's" (ID p. 47–48).
- Complaint counsel did not identify the "wrath of text messages." The only candidate is Sullivan's March 2013 reply "Thanks . . . . Unusual.," which "did not disclose anything regarding Schein's policies" (ID p. 48–49).
- Cohen also testified that Sullivan "never told [him] what Schein's policy was," and that his "understanding" came from "Word on the street. You know, it's a small business. There's no secrets." (ID p. 49).
- "In this regard, the case against Schein is considerably weaker than that against Patterson, in which the evidence included an email from Cohen to Guggenheim expressly stating Benco's policy and Guggenheim's response indicating agreement" (ID p. 50).

> **Characterization difference.** Complaint counsel presents Cohen's IHT answer as showing Benco "gained the understanding" from Sullivan in 2011 (CCB p. 37–38). The ALJ notes the question was about Cohen's understanding in **September 2013**, a "temporal disconnect" (ID p. 48).

### 3.2 Did Sullivan change his attitude in 2011?

**Complaint counsel.** "As of late 2010 and early 2011, Schein's President, Tim Sullivan, was in favor of the company working with buying groups." It relies on Sullivan's 2010 email ("[I don't] want to lose" Smile Source) and his February 2011 letter ("very excited"). "By July 2011, Sullivan's position had changed: 'I don't think you will ever see a full service dealer get involved with GPOs.'" December 2011: "NOT want to lead"; "a very slippery slope"; not "the first company to open the floodgates to the dangerous world of GPOs." February 2012: "what we can do to KILL the buying group model!!" (CCB p. 38, citing CX2113, CX2899, CX0185, CX2456, CX2458, CX0199).

**Schein, on "KILL."** Context changes the meaning. Smile Source had just "fire[d]" Schein, and Sullivan wanted to "retain these customers and judge how effective [Smile Source's] buying group model is… what they 'can do to KILL the[ir] buying group model and retain those member customers'" (SB p. 40–41). Complaint counsel's own footnote records Sullivan's testimony that he "meant to write 'KILL [their] buying group model,' referring to the buying group Smile Source" (CCB n.226).

**ALJ.** Unpersuasive.
- Sullivan and others were skeptical "for years prior to 2011." In 2002, Muller wrote "we have held a pretty firm line on saying NO to virtually all of them." In 2010, Sullivan wrote "I do not support us opening Buying Clubs" and was "not interested in GPOs" (ID p. 56).
- The 2010 Smile Source email, read in full, shows Sullivan reporting that "neither of us support the concept of buying groups," while being "inclined to 'allow'" a test (ID p. 58–59).
- The "very excited" letter is "more logically interpreted as an effort at good customer relations" (ID p. 59 n.25).
- "Still" in "still of the position that we do NOT want to lead" "contradicts the inference that Sullivan was expressing a change of position" (ID p. 60).
- The decision lists "KILL" among statements Schein did *not* "persuasively explain away" (ID p. 62, F. 273). It still holds that such statements are outweighed by conduct (§ 3.4 below).

> **Characterization difference.** "KILL the buying group model" (complaint counsel) versus "KILL the[ir] buying group model" (Schein). The bracket is Schein's insertion. The underlying exhibit (CX0199) should be read to see which reading the surrounding text supports.

### 3.3 Foley's "we no longer participate in Buying Groups" (Dec. 21, 2011)

**The words:** "[U]nless you have some 'ownership' of your practices Henry Schein considers your business model as a Buying Group, and we no longer participate in Buying Groups" (to Unified Smiles; CCB p. 39, citing CX2062; ID p. 60).

**Complaint counsel.** A Schein executive "acknowledging Schein's no buying group policy." Schein rejected Unified Smiles "pursuant to its change in policy and consistent with its prior exchange of assurances with Benco" (CCB p. 39, 41).

**Schein.**
- Unified Smiles "had zero customers" and "did not [even] exist," yet its founder demanded the pricing of Schein's fifth-largest DSO customer (SB p. 35–36).
- Foley called the line "poorly worded." Special Markets *was* participating in buying groups, and Foley decided alone, consulting no one (SB p. 36, citing Foley, Tr. 4691).
- The next day, senior HSD executives met to discuss the MeritDent buying group, which signed on February 7, 2012 (SB, MeritDent section).

**ALJ.**
- The email was to a customer and "must be viewed in that context."
- Complaint counsel "assigns undue probative weight" to it. "In context and fully explained, it carries little weight" (ID p. 60–61).
- The MeritDent proposal went out the day after the Foley email (ID p. 61).

### 3.4 Schein's internal statements versus Schein's conduct

**Statements complaint counsel relied on.** These are as quoted in ID p. 62 and CCB p. 39–40:
- "So, this is a corporate decision, not to participate in these" (Foley, Feb. 2012).
- "Neither HSD or Special Markets will participate in buying groups of any kind" (zone manager, Apr. 2012).
- "against what Tim Sullivan has directed us to do in regards to supporting Buying groups" (July 2012).
- "We try to avoid buying groups at all costs" (May 2013).
- "from Tim S, HSD does not want to enter the GPO world" (July 2014).
- "I think the meta msg is officially, GPO's are not good for Schein" (Titus, July 2014).
- "we are NOT participating in any GPOs regardless of what they promise to bring us" (Oct. 2014).

**Schein's explanation.** "Buying group" meant a "price-only" group that could not "drive compliance" (SB p. 15–16 as summarized ID p. 63). Titus testified (Tr. Vol. 18, Feb. 8, 2019, `d09379bencotrialvol18.pdf`, around Tr. 5229):
- On "meta message": "perhaps not a very thoughtful choice of words, but I was specifically referring to a group like this, a start-up that did not put their money where their mouth is… and could not enforce compliance."
- Asked whether she meant Schein would not work with GPOs: "I was not. In fact, that would be counter to reality, which I was working with many GPOs."
- On "It went to Tim and he shot it down": "I never spoke to Tim. He did not tell me that," and "I don't like the… words I use, 'shot it down.'"
- Sullivan never told her not to work with buying groups: "Absolutely not."
- The PGMS decision was "a consensus amongst our management and leadership group" and had "Nothing whatsoever" to do with Benco or Patterson.
- In an April 2014 email: "I am not 100 percent against GPOs as long as they work for us."

**Complaint counsel's reply.** "Schein executives never distinguished between types of buying groups during the conspiracy… they rejected buying groups categorically." It cites Muller: a buying group "is primarily just price." It also cites Meadows, who did not recall the term "price-only buying group" (CCRB p. 161). Schein's buying-group deals were "cheating" or "imperfect compliance" (ID p. 65, summarizing CCB at 99–101).

**ALJ.**
- "Schein fails to persuasively explain away all the statements," and "some of Schein's statements are suggestive of a policy of avoiding buying groups" (ID p. 61–62).
- Even so, "substantial probative evidence conflicts with the assertion that Schein had a blanket policy." Qualifiers in Schein's own documents support the selective reading: "much more than" a buying group, "nothing more" than a buying group, "classic," "not a pure buying group," "straight out GPO." So does a buying-group founder's testimony that Schein wanted "certain types" but not "pure buying groups" (ID p. 63).
- **The decisive point was conduct.**
  - Schein **kept** Advantage Dental, Breakaway, Comfort Dental, Dentists for a Better Huntington, Long Island Dental Forum and Universal Dental Alliance.
  - It **added** Intermountain Dental Associates, Klear Impakt, MeritDent, the Schulman Group and Dental Gator during the alleged period.
  - It **pursued** Smile Source with a "partnership proposal" in March 2014, while Benco told Smile Source no in February 2014 and Patterson wrote "we have said no to [S]mile [S]ource. They are [a] buying club" (ID p. 63–65).
- On the two terminations complaint counsel cited (Dental Co-Op of Utah, Steadfast), "there is no logical reason to assign more probative weight to Schein's rejecting or terminating some buying groups than to Schein's adding or maintaining other buying groups" (ID p. 64 n.26).
- Calling the deals "cheating" "requires improperly presuming a prior agreement" (ID p. 65, citing *McWane*, *Blomkest*).
- Schein's in-house work on a buying-group strategy (the 2010 guidelines, 2014 due-diligence questions, a January 2015 "HSD GPO response plan," and the 2016 Alternative Purchasing Channel) "is contrary to a conclusion that Schein had a meeting of the minds with Benco" (ID p. 66–67).

> **Characterization difference.** Complaint counsel reads Schein's buying-group deals as cheating on an agreement. Schein and the ALJ read them as evidence that there was no agreement. That difference turns on whether a prior agreement was first proved, and against Schein the ALJ found it was not (§ 3.1).

### 3.6 March 2013 ADC: "reassurance," or an unwelcome disclosure?

**The words.**
- Cohen's calendar for March 25, 2013 read: "Call Tim Sullivan re: Buying Groups" (ID p. 75).
- After an 8-minute call, Sullivan texted: "Yes, I am good with the terms we discussed and I look forward to joining Team Benco! PS. Want to confirm that the Benco tooth logo will include a picture of me. :)"
- That evening, after Cohen sent a link to ADC's press release, Sullivan replied: "Thanks for the follow up on that article. Unusual."
- March 27, Cohen: "Tim: Did some additional research on the Atlantic Care deal, seems like they have actually merged ownership of all the practices. So it's not a buying group, it's a big group. We're going to bid. Thanks." (ID p. 76).

**Complaint counsel.** Cohen was reassuring Sullivan of Benco's compliance (CCB at 33–35). Sullivan's rebuff "cannot be credited":
- He "thanked Cohen for the call and joked with Cohen," thanked him again, and "tried to reengage by calling Cohen two times."
- He "never reported or documented his communications with Cohen… to Schein's legal department… as he was required to do under Schein's antitrust policy."
- "Cohen's testimony about these communications flatly contradicts Sullivan's story" (CCRB p. 207–208).
- Cohen admitted that telling his rival Benco would bid "may be" against Benco's interest (CCB p. 45).

**Schein.** Sullivan: "I immediately stopped him, and said, 'Chuck, this is not a discussion you and I should be having'… [and] I cut off discussion on that topic." "This is exactly what Mr. Sullivan was supposed to do" (SB p. 12, citing Sullivan, Tr. 3946, and *Citric Acid*). The April 3 call was to say it again: "Chuck, you cannot be sending information on what Benco's doing with customers" (ID p. 77).

**Benco.** Cohen sought facts about ADC's ownership to apply Benco's own policy. "Going to bid" "did reveal Benco's plans" but "does not evidence a pre-existing agreement" (BB p. 34–35). The joke referred to the companies' pending merger talks (BB p. 35 n.12).

**ALJ.** Sullivan's account was "credible and convincing" (ID p. 78).
- The joking reflected the April 1, 2013 merger meeting, since Sullivan "might be working for Cohen, or vice versa."
- Sullivan's IHT and deposition match his trial testimony.
- Cohen's "could not recall" "is not the equivalent of denying or contradicting Sullivan's recollections" (ID p. 77–78).
- Cohen's disclosure "can be probative of conspiracy, as an action against self-interest," but "in contrast to Patterson, who solicited Benco to justify Benco's bidding on ADC… the evidence here does not show that Sullivan solicited any information." So "there was no 'exchange'" (ID p. 78). Treating it as reassurance "would require improperly assuming that such prior assurances had been given" (ID p. 79).
- "Whatever Cohen's unsolicited sharing of competitively sensitive information says about Cohen or Benco, it is not convincing evidence against Schein" (ID p. 85).

> **Characterization difference.** The same texts ("Thanks… Unusual," "Team Benco," Sullivan's return calls) are read by complaint counsel as engagement and by Schein and the ALJ as cordiality during merger talks. One more point: complaint counsel says Cohen's testimony "flatly contradicts" Sullivan's (CCRB p. 208), while the ALJ characterizes it as non-recollection. Cohen's own transcript (Tr. 721, and the testimony CCRB cites) is not in this set and should be checked.

### 3.7 "Overarching" agreement: plus factors and statements about all three

- **Parallel conduct.** None as to Schein. Its approach was "selective," "dissimilar to the conduct of both Benco and Patterson" (ID p. 82–83).
- **Acts against interest.** Dr. Marshall's opinion assumed Schein refused buying groups from 2011 to 2015. That premise failed, so the opinion was "properly rejected for this reason" (ID p. 84–85, citing *Brooke Group*).
- **Change of conduct.** Rejected. Schein's 2017 return to Smile Source followed a 2014 bid, and Smile Source's Maurer wrote that Schein "was head and shoulders above the rest… specifically because of Tim Sullivan" (ID p. 86).
- **Motive and "slippery slope."** Consistent with "oligopolistic interdependence" (ID p. 87–88).
- **Statements about the "Big Three."** These include Ryan's "all of the major dental companies have said, 'NO'" and "I already KNOW that Patterson and Schein have said NO," Rogan's "always said no," Foley's "Schein, PDCO and Benco all refused to bid" on Tralongo, and Foley's March 2014 statement to a DSO that the three "are on the same page." The ALJ treated each as a belief about observable past behavior, not knowledge of an agreement: Ryan called his statement about Schein "pure speculation," Ryan's "KNOW" statement was wrong, and Foley "disclaimed any personal knowledge" (ID p. 89–91). *B&R Supermarket* was distinguished: "Confidently predicting what your competitors are going to do in the future is readily distinguishable from statements… about what competitors are presently doing, or have done in the past" (ID p. 90).
- **2014 Texas Dental Association meeting.** The Misiak–Steck exchange is "far too tenuous" to carry significant weight (ID p. 95).

## 4. Why it came out differently for Schein: the ALJ's reasons, side by side

| Element | Patterson | Schein |
|---|---|---|
| **Exchange of assurances** | A written exchange: Cohen's policy statement and Guggenheim's "We feel the same way about these" (ID p. 26). | None proved for 2011. Cohen's "I believe I did" conflicts with Sullivan's denials, and nothing corroborates it (ID p. 47–48, 50). |
| **Who reached out** | Both sides: Cohen in February, Guggenheim in June ("Reflecting back on our conversation…"). | Benco only. "Unsolicited," "rebuffed" (ID p. 73, 78). |
| **Confrontation / reassurance** | Patterson confronted Benco, and Benco reassured it in writing: "As we've discussed, we don't recognize buying groups" (ID p. 36–38). | Benco's contacts can't be confrontations or reassurances without a prior agreement. Reading them that way "improperly requires presuming" one (ID p. 74, 79). |
| **Conduct after contact** | NMDC dropped three days later. A "near blanket refusal" without evaluation. A change after April 2015 (ID p. 27, 49, 47–48). | Kept most legacy groups, added new ones, bid for Smile Source after Benco's October 2013 call, and built buying-group programs internally (ID p. 63–67, 72–73). |
| **Internal "no" statements** | Not explained away. They corroborate the exchange (ID p. 42). | Also not all explained away, but "conflict[s]" with conduct. Qualifiers suggest selectivity (ID p. 61–63). |
| **Witness credibility** | Denials given "little weight" against the documents (ID p. 51). | Sullivan "credible and convincing" (ID p. 78). Foley's and McFadden's explanations accepted (ID p. 61, 98). |

The ALJ's own summary: "Although there are some statements in some documents that could be interpreted as consistent with Complaint Counsel's conspiracy theory, and Benco's apparent willingness to reach out to Schein raises concerns, the evidence as a whole does not hold up under reasonable scrutiny" (ID § II.E.6).

**My observation (inference, not the decision's words).** The Patterson finding rests heavily on one inference that has no direct evidence behind it: that headquarters caused the February 11 withdrawal from NMDC. Patterson attacks exactly that point. On the Schein side, the outcome rests on credibility findings for Sullivan and Foley, and on treating Schein's buying-group deals as inconsistent with agreement rather than as cheating. Both are fact-finder calls that complaint counsel disputes (CCRB p. 207–208, 161). If the partner wants to know where the decision is most exposed on review, those are the points.
