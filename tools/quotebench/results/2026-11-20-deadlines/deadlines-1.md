# Deadlines Reference: FRCP and Title 9 (FAA)

Prepared from the copies of the rules and statute held in this matter. **Every quotation below is taken from these documents, not from memory, and has been checked word-for-word against the document text** (line-break hyphens and running page headers removed; omissions shown as "…").

| Source | File in the matter | Edition shown in the document |
|---|---|---|
| Federal Rules of Civil Procedure, incl. Supplemental Rules for Admiralty (A–G) and for Social Security Actions | `uscourts-federal-rules-of-civil-procedure.pdf` | "as amended to December 1, 2025" (House Judiciary Committee Print No. 4) |
| Title 9, U.S. Code — Arbitration (chs. 1–4) | `usc-title9-arbitration-2023.pdf` (a second copy, `usc-title9-arbitration-2023.htm`, has identical text in every provision quoted here) | Office of the Law Revision Counsel print; current through the 2022 amendments (Pub. L. 117–90) |

**How to read the tables.** *Limit* = the period. *Clock starts* = the triggering event (or the event counted back from). *Applies to* = the act or paper. *Binds* = who must act within the limit. Where the rule is written in the passive voice and the person bound is not named, the *Binds* entry is my reading and is marked "(inferred)".

**What this does not cover.** These are only the limits that appear in these two documents. Local rules, standing orders, the Federal Rules of Appellate Procedure (e.g., the notice-of-appeal period), and statutes outside Title 9 are not in these documents and are not included. Whether a given limit can be extended, waived, or excused is noted only where the text itself says so; case law on those questions was not consulted.

## 11. Limits tied to an event, or stated without a fixed period

These set *when* something must happen without giving a number of days. The team will want them on the same checklist. Grouped by stage; all are in `uscourts-federal-rules-of-civil-procedure.pdf` unless marked T9 (`usc-title9-arbitration-2023.pdf`).

**Service and filing**
- Rule 4(c)(1): plaintiff must serve "within the time allowed by Rule 4(m)".
- Rule 4(i)(4): "The court must allow a party a reasonable time to cure its failure to:" serve all required federal recipients.
- Rule 5(d)(1)(A): papers after the complaint "must be filed no later than a reasonable time after service"; most discovery papers "must not be filed until they are used in the proceeding or the court orders filing".
- Rule 5.1(a): a party drawing a statute's constitutionality into question "must promptly" file and serve the notice.

**Pleadings and Rule 12**
- Rule 12(b): "A motion asserting any of these defenses must be made before pleading if a responsive pleading is allowed."
- Rule 12(c): "After the pleadings are closed—but early enough not to delay trial—a party may move for judgment on the pleadings."
- Rule 12(h)(1): defenses under Rule 12(b)(2)–(5) are waived by omitting them from a Rule 12 motion or failing to raise them "in a responsive pleading or in an amendment allowed by Rule 15(a)(1) as a matter of course."
- Rule 12(h)(2)–(3): failure to state a claim may be raised as late as "at trial"; lack of subject-matter jurisdiction "at any time".
- Rule 15(b)(2): a party may move "at any time, even after judgment" to conform the pleadings to the evidence.
- Rule 41(a)(1)(A)(i): voluntary dismissal by notice only "before the opposing party serves either an answer or a motion for summary judgment".

**Parties**
- Rule 17(a)(3): no dismissal for the wrong real party in interest "until, after an objection, a reasonable time has been allowed for the real party in interest to ratify, join, or be substituted into the action."
- Rule 24(a), (b): intervention requires a "timely motion".

**Discovery**
- Rule 26(e)(1)(A): supplementation "in a timely manner".
- Rule 29(b): "a stipulation extending the time for any form of discovery must have court approval if it would interfere with the time set for completing discovery, for hearing a motion, or for trial."
- Rule 32(d)(1), (2), (4): objections to a deposition notice must be "promptly served in writing"; to the officer's qualification "before the deposition begins" or "promptly after the basis for disqualification becomes known"; to transcription errors, by a motion to suppress "made promptly after the error or irregularity becomes known".
- Rule 45(d)(3)(A)(i): the court must quash or modify a subpoena that "fails to allow a reasonable time to comply".

**Injunctions**
- Rule 65(b)(3): after an ex parte TRO, the preliminary-injunction motion "must be set for hearing at the earliest possible time".

**Trial**
- Rule 49(a)(3): a jury trial on an unsubmitted issue is waived "unless, before the jury retires, the party demands its submission to the jury."
- Rule 50(a)(2): JMOL "may be made at any time before the case is submitted to the jury."
- Rule 51(a)(1): requests for jury instructions "At the close of the evidence or at any earlier reasonable time that the court orders"; Rule 51(c)(2)(B): if a party was not told of an instruction in time, it must object "promptly after learning that the instruction or request will be, or has been, given or refused."

**Judgment**
- Rule 62(b): "At any time after judgment is entered, a party may obtain a stay by providing a bond or other security."
- Rule 77(d)(1)–(2): the clerk must serve notice of entry "Immediately after entering an order or judgment". However, "Lack of notice of the entry does not affect the time for appeal or relieve—or authorize the court to relieve—a party for failing to appeal within the time allowed, except as allowed by Federal Rule of Appellate Procedure (4)(a)."

**Special proceedings**
- Rule 71.1(f): the condemnor may amend "at any time before the trial on compensation".
- Supp. Rule E(4)(f): a person claiming arrested or attached property "shall be entitled to a prompt hearing".
- Supp. Rule G(4)(a)(i): forfeiture notice must be published "within a reasonable time after filing the complaint or at a time the court orders".
- Supp. Rule G(8)(c)(i): "At any time before trial, the government may move to strike a claim or answer".

**Arbitration (T9)**
- § 3: the court shall "stay the trial of the action until such arbitration has been had", provided the applicant "is not in default in proceeding with such arbitration".
- § 205: removal of Convention cases "at any time before the trial thereof" (also in § 2 above).

## Notes and open points

1. **Excluded items found in the documents.** The front matter of the FRCP file reprints 28 U.S.C. § 2074. It says the Supreme Court must transmit new rules to Congress "not later than May 1" and that a rule "shall take effect no earlier than December 1" of that year. That binds the rulemaking process, not litigants, so it is left out of the tables. Rule 4(c)(2) and Rule 45(b)(1) ("at least 18 years old") set an age requirement, not a time limit, so they are also left out. Rule 45(b)(1)'s "fees for 1 day’s attendance" is a fee measure, not a deadline, and is left out too.
2. **Title 9 — mandatory or permissive?** Section 9 says a party "may apply" within one year, and § 207 says "within three years". Section 12 says notice "must be served" within three months. Whether a period is jurisdictional, mandatory, or can be extended is a legal question these two documents don't answer. The matter also holds court opinions (for example, `scotus-smith-v-spizzirri-2024.pdf` and `scotus-coinbase-v-suski-2024.pdf`) that I did not review for this memo.
3. **Title 9 sends some timing back to the FRCP.** Section 4 says service of the petition "shall be made in the manner provided by the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure". Section 6 says any application "shall be made and heard in the manner provided by law for the making and hearing of motions". So Rule 6(c) motion-timing may matter for petitions to compel, confirm or vacate. That is an inference; Title 9 does not say so in terms.
4. **Two Title 9 copies.** The PDF and the HTML copy match, word for word, in every provision quoted here (checked by direct text comparison).
5. **Method.** I split the full rule text (Rules 1–87, Supplemental Rules A–G, Social Security Rules 1–8) into subdivisions. I then extracted every subdivision that mentions days, weeks, months, years or hours, plus every one using "within the time/period", "before …", "after …", "no later than", "promptly", "reasonable time", "timely", "at any time", "immediately", "forthwith", "expires" or "as soon as". I read each one, along with all of Rule 6 and all of Title 9. A limit worded in some other way could still have been missed. The four scanned documents in the matter that could not be text-extracted are unrelated to these sources (they are a discrepancy report, a proof of delivery and a claim letter).
