# Deadlines in the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure and Title 9, U.S. Code

This reference lists 293 time limits set by the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure (as amended to December 1, 2025, including the Supplemental Rules and the authority provisions of 28 U.S.C. §§ 2072–2074 printed with them) and by Title 9 of the U.S. Code (arbitration), grouped by stage of the case. Each entry gives the rule or section, the period, what starts the clock, what it applies to and who it binds, followed by the language that sets the limit, quoted from and cited to the document's file name.

How to read it:

- The quotation under each entry is the document's own text. The fields above it (period, clock, applies to, binds, court may change, notes) are our paraphrase, made to help find the right rule; rely on the quotation and the full rule, not the paraphrase.
- Time limits include fixed periods, "before/after" sequence requirements and non-numeric standards (promptly, timely, a reasonable time, at any time before …). Fixed periods are computed under Rule 6(a), with 3 days added under Rule 6(d) after some kinds of service; see the first section.
- "Court may change" records only what the quoted rule itself allows (a court order setting another time, a stipulation, good cause). Rule 6(b) separately lets the court extend most periods, except those Rule 6(b)(2) names.
- Title 9 is in the matter twice (usc-title9-arbitration-2023.pdf and .htm); the copies were compared limit by limit and agree, so the PDF is quoted.
- Provisions read but not listed as time limits are cited, with the reason, in the last section.
- This is a finding aid compiled from these two documents only; it does not cover local rules, the Federal Rules of Appellate Procedure, other statutes or case law on these periods.

::q{#r6c2b}

## Read but not listed as time limits

These provisions were picked out while reading because they mention timing, but they set no time within which anyone must act.

- **Fed. R. Civ. P. 3** :cite{#r3}: Says when an action is commenced (on filing); it is the trigger for other periods, not a limit itself.
- **Fed. R. Civ. P. 5.1(d)** :cite{#r5-1d}: Says a missed Rule 5.1 notice does not forfeit the claim; it sets no time.
- **Fed. R. Civ. P. 12(h)(1)(B)** :cite{#r12h1-waiver}: A waiver rule keyed to the first Rule 12 motion or responsive pleading, with no period of its own.
- **Fed. R. Civ. P. 13(a)(1)** :cite{#r13a1-time-of-service}: Fixes the moment at which a counterclaim is judged compulsory, not a time to act.
- **Fed. R. Civ. P. 13(a)(2)(A)** :cite{#r13a2a-when-commenced}: Fixes the moment at which another pending action is measured, not a time to act.
- **Fed. R. Civ. P. 26(a)(1)(A)** :cite{#r26a1a-nowait}: Says initial disclosures need not await a request; the timing is in Rule 26(a)(1)(C)–(D).
- **Fed. R. Civ. P. 26(a)(2)(B)(iv)** :cite{#r26a2b-10y}: A look-back period for what an expert report must list, not a deadline.
- **Fed. R. Civ. P. 26(a)(2)(B)(v)** :cite{#r26a2b-4y}: A look-back period for what an expert report must list, not a deadline.
- **Fed. R. Civ. P. 37(a)(5)(A)(i)** :cite{#r37a5a-before}: A condition on expenses (the motion came before a good-faith attempt), not a time limit.
- **Fed. R. Civ. P. 52(a)(1)** :cite{#r52a1-close-evidence}: Says when findings may be stated, not a time anyone must act by.
- **Fed. R. Civ. P. 52(c)** :cite{#r52c-until-close}: A permission for the court to wait, not a limit.
- **Fed. R. Civ. P. 53(b)(2)** :cite{#r53b2-diligence}: A standard of diligence for masters, with no period.
- **Fed. R. Civ. P. 53(g)(1)** :cite{#r53g1-before-or-after}: Says compensation may be fixed before or after judgment; no limit.
- **Fed. R. Civ. P. 57** :cite{#r57-speedy}: The court may order a speedy hearing; no period.
- **Fed. R. Civ. P. 64(a)** :cite{#r64a-throughout}: Says when state seizure remedies are available, not a time limit.
- **Fed. R. Civ. P. 71.1(j)(2)** :cite{#r71-1j2-expedite}: A general direction to expedite, with no period.
- **Fed. R. Civ. P. 77(a)** :cite{#r77a-always-open}: Says the court is always open for filing; it bears on computing time (see Rule 6(a)(3)) but sets no limit.
- **Fed. R. Civ. P. 77(c)(1)** :cite{#r77c1-hours}: The clerk's office hours; relevant to Rule 6(a)(4) but not a limit.
- **Supp. R. E(9)(a)(i)(C)** :cite{#suppe9a-delay}: Unreasonable delay is a ground for an interlocutory sale, not a time limit.
- **Supp. R. G(7)(c)** :cite{#suppg7c-upon}: Says what happens on entry of a forfeiture judgment; no time to act.
- **9 U.S.C. § 13(a)** :cite{#s13-ext}: Lists papers to file with a motion (including any written extension of the time to make the award); the time is the agreement's, not the statute's.
- **9 U.S.C. § 14** :cite{#s14-1926}: An applicability cut-off by contract date (January 1, 1926), not a litigation deadline.
- **9 U.S.C. § 306(a)** :cite{#s306-1988}: Identifies the rules of procedure by date (July 1, 1988), not a deadline.
