*Prepared 2026-11-20 for the litigation team, from the two sources in the matter. Every quotation below was checked word for word against the matter's text of the source. This is a working reference, not legal advice. Confirm each period against the current rule, any local rule and any order in the case before relying on it.*

## How to read this

- **Grouping.** Entries are grouped by stage of the case. Within each stage they run in rule order. Rules that apply at every stage come first: computing time, service and filing, extensions, and the Rule 87 emergency rules.
- **Fixed periods.** Each stage lists its fixed periods first, meaning limits stated as a number of hours, days, weeks, months or years. Each entry gives:
  - the length of the period;
  - what starts the clock;
  - what the limit applies to;
  - who it binds;
  - the language that sets it;
  - the rule or section and the file name.
- **Timing requirements without a fixed period.** These follow in a table. They include "promptly", "reasonable time", "before trial", "at any time", "timely", and periods measured by an event.
- **Quotations.** Quotes reproduce the source text exactly. That includes line-break hyphens from the PDF (for example "ad- mitted"). Where a running page header splits a sentence in the source, it is marked **[page header]** and the header text is left out. That marker is the only editorial insertion.
- **"Clock", "Applies to", "Binds", "Can change", "Note".** These are summaries written for this reference, not quotations. Where a note leans on another rule, that rule's own entry is in this reference. Two exceptions:
  - The § 205 note mentions 28 U.S.C. § 1446, which is not in the matter.
  - The § 9 note flags a question this reference does not answer.

## What this reference does not cover

- **Other sources.** Several periods that matter in federal litigation come from outside these two documents, and the matter does not contain them:
  - the time to appeal (Fed. R. App. P. 4; Rule 77(d)(2) points there);
  - the time to remove (28 U.S.C. § 1446);
  - statutes of limitations;
  - local rules, standing orders and the case's scheduling order.

  Check these separately.
- **Not time limits.** Provisions with no timing element were left out. Examples:
  - the 100-mile subpoena radius;
  - the minimum age of a process server;
  - jury size;
  - interest rates in the Supplemental Rules;
  - the 10-year and 4-year look-backs that set what an expert report must list (Rule 26(a)(2)(B)(iv)–(v));
  - dates that only fix when a rule or treaty took effect.
- **Rule 4 forms.** The Rule 4 waiver forms (Notice of a Lawsuit, Waiver of Service, Duty to Avoid Unnecessary Expenses) repeat the Rule 4(d) periods. They appear only under Rule 4(d).

## How it was compiled

- **Reading.** The rules were split by Title into six parts, with Title 9 as a seventh. Each part was read in full by a separate reader, who logged every provision setting a time limit.
- **Quote check.** Every quotation was then checked by script against the matter's text of the source: 437 of 437 match.
- **Gap check.** A scan of the full text looked for numeric periods (hours, days, weeks, months, years) not covered by any quotation. It found only:
  - the server's minimum age (Rule 4(c)(2));
  - the Rule 4 waiver form text.

  A second scan for "promptly", "forthwith", "immediately", "reasonable time", "timely", "no later than", "at any time" and "before trial" found nothing material missing.
- **Extraction limits.**
  - Non-numeric timing language is judgment-laden. The tables lean toward including it, and they may still miss an instance phrased some other way.
  - The rules text is extracted from the PDF. Four documents in the matter could not be read (scanned images, no OCR available), but neither source is among them.

## 1. Time computation, service & filing (all stages)

**Fixed periods**

- **Rule 6(c)(1) — at least 14 days before the hearing.** Clock: time specified for the hearing. Applies to: service of written motion and notice of hearing. Binds: moving party.
  > “A written motion and notice of the hearing must be served at least 14 days before the time specified for the hearing, with the following exceptions:”
  Source: Rule 6(c)(1), `uscourts-federal-rules-of-civil-procedure.pdf`. Can change: exceptions: ex parte motions; "when these rules set a different time"; "when a court order ... sets a different time". Note: Court order setting different time may be applied for ex parte for good cause.
- **Rule 6(c)(2) — at least 7 days before the hearing.** Clock: hearing. Applies to: opposing affidavit. Binds: opposing party.
  > “any opposing affidavit must be served at least 7 days before the hearing, unless the court permits service at another time.”
  Source: Rule 6(c)(2), `uscourts-federal-rules-of-civil-procedure.pdf`. Can change: "unless the court permits service at another time"; Rule 59(c) provides otherwise. Note: Rule 59(c) governs affidavits on new-trial motions.
- **Rule 6(d) — 3 days added.** Clock: service by mail, leaving with clerk, or other consented means. Applies to: time to act after being served. Binds: party that must act after service.
  > “3 days are added after the period would otherwise expire under Rule 6(a).”
  Source: Rule 6(d), `uscourts-federal-rules-of-civil-procedure.pdf`. Note: Applies to service under Rule 5(b)(2)(C), (D), or (F); not electronic service.
- **Rule 87(b)(1)(C) — no more than 90 days.** Clock: declaration of Civil Rules emergency. Applies to: duration of emergency declaration. Binds: Judicial Conference.
  > “must be limited to a stated period of no more than 90 days.”
  Source: Rule 87(b)(1)(C), `uscourts-federal-rules-of-civil-procedure.pdf`. Note: Additional declarations may be issued (87(b)(3)).
- **Rule 87(c)(2)(A) — no more than 30 days.** Clock: entry of the order. Applies to: extension of time to act under Rules 50(b) and (d), 52(b), 59(b), (d), and (e), and 60(b). Binds: court (extension); movants.
  > “A court may, by order, apply Rule 6(b)(1)(A) to extend for a period of no more than 30 days after entry of the order the time to act under Rules 50(b) and (d), 52(b), 59(b), (d), and (e), and 60(b).”
  Source: Rule 87(c)(2)(A), `uscourts-federal-rules-of-civil-procedure.pdf`. Can change: court extends by order under Rule 6(b)(1)(A). Note: Only during a declared emergency.

## 2. Commencement & service

**Fixed periods**

- **Rule 4(d)(1)(F) — at least 30 days.** Clock: request for waiver sent. Applies to: time given defendant to return waiver (defendant in U.S.). Binds: plaintiff (must give); defendant (to return).
  > “give the defendant a reasonable time of at least 30 days after the request was sent”
  Source: Rule 4(d)(1)(F), `uscourts-federal-rules-of-civil-procedure.pdf`. Note: Waiver request content requirement.
- **Rule 4(d)(1)(F) — at least 60 days.** Clock: request for waiver sent to defendant outside any judicial district. Applies to: time given defendant outside U.S. districts to return waiver. Binds: plaintiff (must give); defendant (to return).
  > “or at least 60 days if sent to the defendant outside any judicial district of the United States”
  Source: Rule 4(d)(1)(F), `uscourts-federal-rules-of-civil-procedure.pdf`.
- **Rule 4(m) — 90 days.** Clock: after the complaint is filed. Applies to: service of summons and complaint on a defendant. Binds: plaintiff; court.
  > “If a defendant is not served within 90 days after the complaint is filed”
  Source: Rule 4(m), `uscourts-federal-rules-of-civil-procedure.pdf`. Can change: court may order service "within a specified time"; must extend "for an appropriate period" on good cause. Note: Court must dismiss without prejudice or order service within specified time.

## 3. Pleadings & pre-answer motions

**Fixed periods**

- **Rule 4(d)(3) — 60 days.** Clock: after waiver request was sent. Applies to: answer to complaint by defendant who timely returns waiver. Binds: defendant.
  > “need not serve an answer to the complaint until 60 days after the re- quest was sent”
  Source: Rule 4(d)(3), `uscourts-federal-rules-of-civil-procedure.pdf`. Note: Defendant must timely return the waiver before being served. The period runs from when the request was sent, not from its return. The Rule 4 waiver forms repeat these periods.
- **Rule 4(d)(3) — 90 days.** Clock: after request was sent to defendant outside any judicial district. Applies to: answer to complaint after waiver. Binds: defendant outside U.S..
  > “or until 90 days after it was sent to the de- fendant outside any judicial district of the United States”
  Source: Rule 4(d)(3), `uscourts-federal-rules-of-civil-procedure.pdf`.
- **Rule 11(c)(2) — 21 days (safe harbor).** Clock: service of the Rule 11 sanctions motion. Applies to: withdrawal/correction of challenged paper before the motion may be filed or presented. Binds: party served with sanctions motion.
  > “it must not be filed or be pre- sented to the court if the challenged paper, claim, defense, contention, or denial is withdrawn or appropriately corrected within 21 days after service or within another time the court sets”
  Source: Rule 11(c)(2), `uscourts-federal-rules-of-civil-procedure.pdf`. Can change: court may set "another time". Note: Motion must be served under Rule 5 but not filed during safe harbor.
- **Rule 12(a)(1)(A)(i) — 21 days.** Clock: service of summons and complaint. Applies to: answer. Binds: defendant.
  > “within 21 days after being served with the sum- mons and complaint”
  Source: Rule 12(a)(1)(A)(i), `uscourts-federal-rules-of-civil-procedure.pdf`. Can change: Unless another time is specified by a federal statute; Rule 12(a)(4) motion effect; "Unless the court sets a different time" (12(a)(4)). Note: Opening lead-in: 'Unless another time is specified by a federal statute'.
- **Rule 12(a)(1)(A)(ii) — 60 days (90 days if defendant outside any judicial district of the U.S.).** Clock: date the request for waiver of service was sent. Applies to: answer, where service timely waived under Rule 4(d). Binds: defendant.
  > “if it has timely waived service under Rule 4(d), within 60 days after the request for a waiver was sent, or within 90 days after it was sent to the defendant outside any judicial district of the United States.”
  Source: Rule 12(a)(1)(A)(ii), `uscourts-federal-rules-of-civil-procedure.pdf`. Note: Two periods in one sentence (60 / 90 days); see also Rule 4(d)(3).
- **Rule 12(a)(1)(B) — 21 days.** Clock: service of the pleading stating the counterclaim or crossclaim. Applies to: answer to counterclaim or crossclaim. Binds: any party.
  > “A party must serve an answer to a counterclaim or crossclaim within 21 days after being served with the pleading that states the counterclaim or crossclaim.”
  Source: Rule 12(a)(1)(B), `uscourts-federal-rules-of-civil-procedure.pdf`.
- **Rule 12(a)(1)(C) — 21 days (unless order specifies different time).** Clock: service of order to reply. Applies to: reply to an answer. Binds: any party.
  > “A party must serve a reply to an answer within 21 days after being served with an order to reply, unless the order specifies a different time.”
  Source: Rule 12(a)(1)(C), `uscourts-federal-rules-of-civil-procedure.pdf`. Can change: "unless the order specifies a different time". Note: Reply allowed only if court orders (Rule 7(a)(7)).
- **Rule 12(a)(2) — 60 days.** Clock: service on the United States attorney. Applies to: answer to complaint, counterclaim, or crossclaim. Binds: United States, U.S. agency, or U.S. officer/employee sued only in official capacity.
  > “must serve an answer to a complaint, coun- terclaim, or crossclaim within 60 days after service on the United States attorney.”
  Source: Rule 12(a)(2), `uscourts-federal-rules-of-civil-procedure.pdf`. Note: Hyphen break in source.
- **Rule 12(a)(3) — 60 days.** Clock: service on the officer or employee or on the United States attorney, whichever is later. Applies to: answer to complaint, counterclaim, or crossclaim. Binds: U.S. officer or employee sued in individual capacity.
  > “within 60 days after service on the officer or employee or service on the United States attorney, whichever is later.”
  Source: Rule 12(a)(3), `uscourts-federal-rules-of-civil-procedure.pdf`. Note: Applies to acts in connection with duties on the United States' behalf.
- **Rule 12(a)(4)(A) — 14 days.** Clock: notice of the court's action denying the Rule 12 motion or postponing disposition until trial. Applies to: responsive pleading. Binds: party that served the Rule 12 motion.
  > “the responsive pleading must be served within 14 days after notice of the court’s action”
  Source: Rule 12(a)(4)(A), `uscourts-federal-rules-of-civil-procedure.pdf`. Can change: "Unless the court sets a different time". Note: Serving a Rule 12 motion alters Rule 12(a) periods.
- **Rule 12(a)(4)(B) — 14 days.** Clock: service of the more definite statement. Applies to: responsive pleading after Rule 12(e) motion granted. Binds: party that served the motion.
  > “the responsive pleading must be served within 14 days after the more definite statement is served.”
  Source: Rule 12(a)(4)(B), `uscourts-federal-rules-of-civil-procedure.pdf`. Can change: "Unless the court sets a different time".
- **Rule 12(e) — 14 days (or time the court sets).** Clock: notice of the order for a more definite statement. Applies to: complying with order for more definite statement. Binds: party ordered to provide it.
  > “If the court orders a more definite statement and the order is not obeyed within 14 days after notice of the order or within the time the court sets, the court may strike the pleading or issue any other appropriate order.”
  Source: Rule 12(e), `uscourts-federal-rules-of-civil-procedure.pdf`. Can change: "or within the time the court sets". Note: Consequence of noncompliance: court may strike pleading.
- **Rule 12(f)(2) — before responding; or 21 days after service if no response allowed.** Clock: service of the pleading. Applies to: motion to strike by a party. Binds: moving party.
  > “on motion made by a party either before responding to the pleading or, if a response is not allowed, within 21 days after being served with the pleading”
  Source: Rule 12(f)(2), `uscourts-federal-rules-of-civil-procedure.pdf`. Note: Court may also act on its own at any time (12(f)(1)).
- **Rule 15(a)(1)(A) — 21 days.** Clock: service of the pleading. Applies to: amendment once as a matter of course. Binds: party.
  > “A party may amend its pleading once as a matter of course no later than: (A) 21 days after serving it, or”
  Source: Rule 15(a)(1)(A), `uscourts-federal-rules-of-civil-procedure.pdf`. Note: Alternative (B) applies where a responsive pleading is required.
- **Rule 15(a)(1)(B) — 21 days (earlier of two events).** Clock: service of a responsive pleading, or service of a Rule 12(b), (e), or (f) motion, whichever is earlier. Applies to: amendment once as a matter of course where responsive pleading required. Binds: party.
  > “21 days after service of a responsive pleading or 21 days after service of a motion under Rule 12(b), (e), or (f), whichever is earlier.”
  Source: Rule 15(a)(1)(B), `uscourts-federal-rules-of-civil-procedure.pdf`.
- **Rule 15(a)(3) — later of time remaining to respond to original, or 14 days after service of amended pleading.** Clock: service of amended pleading. Applies to: response to amended pleading. Binds: party required to respond.
  > “any required response to an amended pleading must be made with- in the time remaining to respond to the original pleading or within 14 days after service of the amended pleading, which- ever is later.”
  Source: Rule 15(a)(3), `uscourts-federal-rules-of-civil-procedure.pdf`. Can change: "Unless the court orders otherwise".
- **Rule 15(c)(1)(C) — within the Rule 4(m) period (90 days after complaint filed, by cross-reference).** Clock: filing of complaint (Rule 4(m) service period). - **Rule 33(b)(2) — 30 days.** Clock: Being served with the interrogatories. Applies to: Serving answers and any objections. Binds: Responding party.
  > “The responding party must serve its an- swers and any objections within 30 days after being served with the interrogatories.”
  Source: Rule 33(b)(2), `uscourts-federal-rules-of-civil-procedure.pdf`. Can change: "A shorter or longer time may be stip- ulated to under Rule 29 or be ordered by the court.".
- **Rule 34(b)(2)(A) — 30 days.** Clock: Being served with the request. Applies to: Written response to a Rule 34 request. Binds: Responding party.
  > “must respond in writing within 30 days after being served”
  Source: Rule 34(b)(2)(A), `uscourts-federal-rules-of-civil-procedure.pdf`. Can change: "A shorter or longer time may be stipulated to under Rule 29 or be ordered by the court.".
- **Rule 34(b)(2)(A) — 30 days.** Clock: Parties' first Rule 26(f) conference (request delivered under Rule 26(d)(2)). Applies to: Written response to an early Rule 34 request. Binds: Responding party.
  > “or—if the request was delivered under Rule 26(d)(2)—within 30 days after the parties’ first Rule 26(f) conference.”
  Source: Rule 34(b)(2)(A), `uscourts-federal-rules-of-civil-procedure.pdf`. Can change: "A shorter or longer time may be stipulated to under Rule 29 or be ordered by the court.".
- **Rule 36(a)(3) — 30 days.** Clock: Being served with the request for admission. Applies to: Serving written answer or objection (matter deemed admitted if not). Binds: Party to whom request is directed.
  > “A matter is ad- mitted unless, within 30 days after being served, the party to [page header] whom the request is directed serves on the requesting party a written answer or objection addressed to the matter and signed by the party or its attorney.”
  Source: Rule 36(a)(3), `uscourts-federal-rules-of-civil-procedure.pdf`. Can change: "A shorter or longer time for responding may be stipulated to under Rule 29 or be or- dered by the court.". Note: Failure to respond in time results in automatic admission; withdrawal only by court on motion (Rule 36(b)).
- **Rule 45(d)(2)(B) — before the earlier of the compliance time or 14 days.** Clock: service of the subpoena. Applies to: written objection to subpoena to produce/inspect. Binds: person commanded to produce or permit inspection.
  > “The objection must be served before the earlier of the time specified for compliance or 14 days after the subpoena is served.”
  Source: Rule 45(d)(2)(B), `uscourts-federal-rules-of-civil-procedure.pdf`. Note: Whichever is earlier.

## 7. Pretrial motions, dismissal & settlement

**Fixed periods**

- **Rule 55(b)(2) — at least 7 days before the hearing.** Clock: hearing on default judgment application. Applies to: Written notice of application for default judgment. Binds: the applying party (to a defaulting party who has appeared).
  > “must be served with written notice of the application at least 7 days before the hearing”
  Source: Rule 55(b)(2), `uscourts-federal-rules-of-civil-procedure.pdf`. Note: Applies only if the defaulting party appeared personally or by a representative.
- **Rule 56(b) — any time until 30 days after close of all discovery.** Clock: close of all discovery. Applies to: Motion for summary judgment. Binds: any party.
  > “Unless a different time is set by local rule or the court orders otherwise, a party may file a motion for summary judgment at any time until 30 days after the close of all discovery”
  Source: Rule 56(b), `uscourts-federal-rules-of-civil-procedure.pdf`. Can change: “Unless a different time is set by local rule or the court orders otherwise”. Note: Outer deadline; motion may be filed at any time before.
- **Rule 68(a) — at least 14 days before the date set for trial.** Clock: trial date. Applies to: Serving an offer of judgment. Binds: a party defending against a claim.
  > “At least 14 days before the date set for trial, a party defending against a claim may serve on an opposing party an offer to allow judgment on specified terms, with the costs then accrued.”
  Source: Rule 68(a), `uscourts-federal-rules-of-civil-procedure.pdf`.
- **Rule 68(a) — 14 days.** Clock: service of the offer. Applies to: Serving written notice accepting an offer of judgment. Binds: the offeree (opposing party).
  > “If, with- in 14 days after being served, the opposing party serves written no- tice accepting the offer”
  Source: Rule 68(a), `uscourts-federal-rules-of-civil-procedure.pdf`. Note: Then either party may file offer, notice and proof of service; clerk must enter judgment.
- **Rule 68(c) — at least 14 days.** Clock: date set for hearing on extent of liability. Applies to: Offer of judgment after liability determined. Binds: the party held liable.
  > “but at least 14 days—before the date set for a hearing to determine the extent of liability”
  Source: Rule 68(c), `uscourts-federal-rules-of-civil-procedure.pdf`.

## 9. Post-trial & judgment

**Fixed periods**

  > “No later than 28 days after the entry of judgment”
  Source: Rule 50(b), `uscourts-federal-rules-of-civil-procedure.pdf`. Note: Court cannot extend (Rule 6(b)(2)); not stated here.
- **Rule 50(b) — no later than 28 days.** Clock: discharge of the jury, where motion addresses a jury issue not decided by a verdict. Applies to: renewed motion for JMOL. Binds: movant.
  > “or if the motion addresses a jury issue not decided by a verdict, no later than 28 days after the jury was discharged”
  Source: Rule 50(b), `uscourts-federal-rules-of-civil-procedure.pdf`.

## 13. Arbitration (Federal Arbitration Act, 9 U.S.C.)

**Fixed periods**

- **9 U.S.C. § 4 — five days' notice (minimum), in writing.** Clock: Service of the petition to compel arbitration; clock counts back from hearing/return day. Applies to: Written notice of petition/application to compel arbitration. Binds: Petitioning party (aggrieved party); served on the party in default.
  > “Five days' notice in writing of such application shall be served upon the party in default.”
  Source: 9 U.S.C. § 4, `usc-title9-arbitration-2023.htm` (same words in `usc-title9-arbitration-2023.pdf`). Note: PDF uses a curly apostrophe (days’); wording otherwise identical. Service per Federal Rules of Civil Procedure. Petition to any U.S. district court with jurisdiction under title 28.
- **9 U.S.C. § 9 — one year (any time within).** Clock: After the award is made. Applies to: Application to confirm award. Binds: Any party to the arbitration.
  > “then at any time within one year after the award is made any party to the arbitration may apply to the court so specified for an order confirming the award”
  Source: 9 U.S.C. § 9, `usc-title9-arbitration-2023.htm` (same words in `usc-title9-arbitration-2023.pdf`). Can change: Only if parties' agreement specifies court and judgment entry; text itself has no extension mechanism. Note: Applies where the parties agreed that a judgment of the court shall be entered on the award. Whether the one-year period is mandatory or permissive is not settled by the text (a case-law question this reference does not address). Chapter 2 (Convention) awards: three years under § 207.
- **9 U.S.C. § 12 — three months.** Clock: After the award is filed or delivered. Applies to: Service of notice of motion to vacate, modify, or correct award. Binds: Moving party, serving the adverse party or his attorney.
  > “Notice of a motion to vacate, modify, or correct an award must be served upon the adverse party or his attorney within three months after the award is filed or delivered.”
  Source: 9 U.S.C. § 12, `usc-title9-arbitration-2023.htm` (same words in `usc-title9-arbitration-2023.pdf`). Can change: Not in text; no tolling or extension provision. Note: Service deadline (notice of motion), not just filing. Applies to motions under §§ 10 and 11. Resident adverse party: serve per law for motion notice; nonresident: by U.S. marshal.
- **9 U.S.C. § 207 — three years.** Clock: After the arbitral award falling under the Convention is made. Applies to: Application to confirm Convention award. Binds: Any party to the arbitration.
  > “Within three years after an arbitral award falling under the Convention is made, any party to the arbitration may apply to any court having jurisdiction under this chapter for an order confirming the award as against any other party to the arbitration.”
  Source: 9 U.S.C. § 207, `usc-title9-arbitration-2023.htm` (same words in `usc-title9-arbitration-2023.pdf`). Can change: Not in text. Note: Chapter 2 (New York Convention); also applies to chapter 3 via § 302.
- **9 U.S.C. § 302 — three years (incorporated from § 207).** Clock: After the Inter-American Convention award is made. Applies to: Application to confirm Inter-American Convention award. Binds: Any party to the arbitration.
  > “Sections 202, 203, 204, 205, and 207 of this title shall apply to this chapter as if specifically set forth herein”
  Source: 9 U.S.C. § 302, `usc-title9-arbitration-2023.htm` (same words in `usc-title9-arbitration-2023.pdf`). Can change: Not in text. Note: Section 302 incorporates the 3-year period of § 207 and the removal timing of § 205 into chapter 3.
