## Where the documents disagree — summary

1. **Was it delivered?** Carrier and Harrowgate: delivered 2/24 at 14:47, signed "TK" (:cite{#carrier}, :cite{#shiplog}, :cite{#dl1}). Bellwether: never received against the PO (:cite{#zoe0309}, :cite{#resp2}, :cite{#depornever}) — yet its own records show a pallet with "HFS" wrap physically at Dock 4 from 2/24 to 3/4 (:cite{#log0224}, :cite{#tk0310b}, :cite{#depo8}). The legal question is whether a quarantined, unlabelled pallet is "Delivery" under §5 and could be refused under §6.
2. **Who signed "TK"?** Ibáñez, per Bellwether's camera notes, log and RDR as read at deposition (:cite{#cam1}, :cite{#log0224}, :cite{#deporrdr}); Kettering was offsite (:cite{#roster}, :cite{#depooff}). Harrowgate initially assumed Kettering (:cite{#dana0313}). The receipt itself is unread.
3. **Was the PO / packing list on the pallet?** Bellwether: no (:cite{#resp3}, :cite{#log0224}). Harrowgate: :q{#dana0403b} — Okafor's report of what the warehouse told her; no warehouse record found.
4. **Notice of non-receipt.** Bellwether relies on the 3/2 call (:cite{#resp4}, :cite{#zoe0302}); Okafor's notes say Müller only asked for an ETA (:cite{#dana0302}). The first *written* notice is 3/9 (:cite{#zoe0309}, :cite{#dl309}), after the 3/2 deadline Harrowgate asserts (:cite{#dl72}).
5. **The return.** Bellwether initiated it on 3/2 before any dispute was raised (:cite{#tk0302}); Harrowgate calls it a release of unclaimed freight and argues it proves receipt (:cite{#dl3}, :cite{#dl4}).
6. **Dates.** Storage charged from 3/11 vs pickup 3/4 (:cite{#hold413}, :cite{#log0304}); Kettering's estimate of about a week vs the log's eight days (:cite{#tk0310b}, :cite{#depo8}); a stray 2/9 log row bearing this PO number (:cite{#log0209}).
